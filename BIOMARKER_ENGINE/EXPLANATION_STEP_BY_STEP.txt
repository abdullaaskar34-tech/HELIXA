WHAT THIS FOLDER CONTAINS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
-------------------------------------------
For each of the six molecular subtypes, a ranked list of genes that satisfy
three conditions at once: the gene identifies that subtype in real patients,
a glioblastoma cell dies without it, and a normal cell does not. Every gene
was then looked up in the published literature one at a time, and what came
back — including the bad news — is written next to it with the source URL.
Nothing is deployed to the website. The deliverables are Excel files, figures
and this document.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  START HERE: WHY THE FIRST ATTEMPT FAILED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You already tried this once, in Druggable_Candidates_sonstage. It took the
marker genes of each subtype and asked DepMap "is this gene essential?".
265 genes went in. Ten came out. The MTC subtype got zero.

Those ten were POLR2L, POLR2F, RPLP1, RPL28, PPA1, PCBP2, NSF, MOB4, IFITM3
and C19orf53 — RNA polymerase subunits, ribosomal proteins, a vesicle-fusion
ATPase. Look at figure 02 in 04_Plots. For every one of them the green bar and
the red bar are the same height:

        POLR2L    glioblastoma needs it 1.00    every other cell 1.00
        NSF       glioblastoma needs it 1.00    every other cell 1.00
        RPLP1     glioblastoma needs it 0.97    every other cell 0.99
        PCBP2     glioblastoma needs it 0.94    every other cell 0.98

A drug against POLR2L stops RNA polymerase in the tumour and in the patient's
heart, kidneys and bone marrow at the same rate. These are not targets. They
are the price of being alive.

The root cause, in one sentence:

        A MARKER IS NOT A DEPENDENCY, AND ESSENTIAL IS NOT SELECTIVE.

Two separate mistakes were stacked on top of each other. First, "this gene is
high in this subtype" tells you nothing about whether the tumour needs it.
Second, "essential" was measured without asking essential COMPARED TO WHAT.

1,329 of the 17,931 measured genes are dependencies in essentially every cell
line in existence. That is the trap, and it swallowed the first attempt whole.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1 · WHAT EACH SUBTYPE SWITCHES ON        (01_Engine/01_patient_side.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Data: the 328 real GDC patients, corrected exactly the way the classifier
corrects a new patient — filtered to the 25,738 retained genes, renormalised
to a 1,000,000 budget, log2, then z-scored against the stored mean and SD of
the patient's own library protocol. Residual batch signal afterwards: 0.0000.

Discovery uses only the 273 CORE patients. Boundary patients sit between
subtypes by definition, so including them blurs exactly the contrast being
measured. All 328 are used in the figures.

For each subtype, that subtype against the other five:
    · Welch t-test, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR
    · Cohen's d — with 26 to 57 patients per group a tiny difference can be
      highly significant and still meaningless
    · SPECIFICITY MARGIN, which is the column that does the real work:
      the gene's mean in this subtype minus its mean in the BEST of the other
      five, not their average. A gene high in three subtypes at once is not a
      biomarker for any of them.

Genes passing FDR < 0.05, d > 0.5 and margin > 0.15 z:

        MTC   1,838      PN   9,984      CL   3,070
        MES   2,389      INT 11,391      OLIGO 3,083

Those numbers are large, and that is the point: expression alone cannot narrow
anything down. Step 2 is what makes the list short.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 2 · WHAT A GLIOBLASTOMA CANNOT LIVE WITHOUT
                                             (01_Engine/02_dependency_side.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Completely different data, from a completely different experiment. DepMap has
knocked out every gene in every one of 1,208 human cancer cell lines with
CRISPR and recorded whether the line died. Among them:

        53 glioblastoma / gliosarcoma lines      <- the tumour
        1,118 lines from outside the brain       <- the control

Three numbers per gene, and the second one is the whole idea:

  1  DEPENDENCY        mean score across the 53 glioblastoma lines
  2  THERAPEUTIC WINDOW  the same across the 1,118 non-brain lines.
                       A real target is HIGH in (1) and LOW in (2).
  3  HETEROGENEITY     the spread across the glioblastoma lines. A gene every
                       glioblastoma needs equally cannot be a SUBTYPE marker.
                       A gene some lines cannot live without while others
                       shrug is exactly the shape a subtype-specific
                       vulnerability has.

Figure 03 plots all 17,931 genes on axes (1) and (2). The diagonal is the line
of no therapeutic window. The red cloud in the top-right corner is the 1,329
pan-essential genes: needed by everything, useful against nothing. The first
attempt was fishing in that corner.

Result: 564 genes are selective glioblastoma dependencies with a real window
and meaningful variation between lines.

A BUG THAT THIS DOCUMENT WOULD BE DISHONEST TO OMIT
    The first version of this step counted a gene as "not dependent" in cell
    lines where it had never been measured. 744 of the genes were screened in
    fewer than half the panel, so pan-essential genes looked selective. NPM1
    read as 27% dependent when its true figure is 85%, and it reached the
    candidate list. It was caught by noticing that its mean score (0.90) and
    its dependent-fraction (0.27) could not both be true. The statistics were
    recomputed over measured lines only, a minimum of 100 measured lines was
    imposed, and a second gate (mean dependency outside the brain below 0.60)
    was added. NPM1 no longer appears.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 3 · THE INTERSECTION                        (01_Engine/03_intersect.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Keep only genes that pass BOTH tests. 359 survive.

Ranking uses the GEOMETRIC mean of an identity score and a survival score, not
the arithmetic mean, precisely because the geometric mean punishes imbalance
instead of averaging it away. A spectacular marker with a feeble dependency,
and a beautiful dependency shared by every subtype, both sink.

For comparison: 1,208 genes pass the identity test and are simply "essential"
with no therapeutic-window filter. That is roughly the number the first
attempt was working with, and most of them are pan-essential.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 4 · WHAT THE LITERATURE SAYS       (01_Engine/04_integrate_and_excel.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first three steps are arithmetic. Arithmetic does not know that a gene is
a tumour suppressor.

The top 10 of every subtype — 61 genes — were each looked up individually in
PubMed, the Human Protein Atlas, Open Targets, DrugBank and ClinicalTrials.gov.
288 source URLs are recorded. The verdicts:

        STRONG    12    published glioblastoma evidence AND matching biology
        MODERATE  25    cancer evidence, plausible biology
        WEAK      18    little or no published support
        NOVEL      7    essentially unstudied — interesting, unproven

That check changed three things, and they matter more than the ranking.

  1  FIVE GENES POINT THE WRONG WAY, AND WERE THROWN OUT

     PRKAR1A   tumour suppressor. Inactivating mutations cause Carney complex.
     APC       tumour suppressor. It DEGRADES beta-catenin and switches Wnt
               OFF. Inhibiting it would switch Wnt ON.
     RPL41     tumour suppressor, deleted in ~59% of tumour cell lines — and
               a 2025 in vivo CRISPR screen found it IS required for normal
               tissue to proliferate, overturning its old "nonessential"
               label. It passed every statistical gate. Only reading the
               literature caught it.
     BNIP1     behaves as a tumour suppressor in the one functional study.
     DCTN1     in low-grade glioma, HIGH expression predicts BETTER survival.

     PRKAR1A was ranked FIRST in the INT panel on the numbers alone. If this
     project had shipped a statistics-only pipeline, its headline INT target
     would have been a gene whose inhibition helps the tumour.

  2  THREE MITOCHONDRIAL GENES LANDED IN THE WRONG SUBTYPE
     PISD, SLC25A28 and NDUFAF3 are mitochondrial proteins that surfaced in
     the OLIGO (neural/myelin) panel. Their biology fits MTC. They are flagged
     in a "Flag" column rather than quietly deleted, because it is a genuine
     limitation of ranking on expression: a gene can be statistically specific
     to a subtype whose biology it has nothing to do with.

  3  SOME DRUGS ALREADY EXIST — AND SOME ALREADY FAILED
     Recorded on the same row as the target. See the table above.

  A CORRECTION MADE WHILE BUILDING THIS
     An early version guessed drug status from keywords anywhere in the
     evidence text. It labelled PARN "approved" when PARN's own entry ends
     "Approval status: preclinical / none known", and read "no known ligands,
     drugs or tool compounds" as evidence that a tool compound exists.
     Overclaiming a drug is the worst error this file could contain, so the
     rule was rewritten to take only the explicit status clause the literature
     check wrote, and to claim LESS whenever the wording is ambiguous. After
     the fix, exactly 3 genes claim an approved drug and 40 claim none at all.

FINAL TIERS
    TIER 1 · ACTIONABLE   17   both data gates, published support, a drug
                               that exists, not a tumour suppressor
    TIER 2 · CREDIBLE     18   both data gates and published support,
                               no drug yet
    TIER 3 · HYPOTHESIS   21   both data gates, thin literature. Real
                               findings, unproven.
    EXCLUDED               5   the biology points the wrong way


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHY THE RESULTS ARE BIOLOGICALLY CREDIBLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The strongest argument that this pipeline works is not any single number. It
is that the genes it returns match the subtypes they were assigned to, without
anyone telling it to.

  MES came back with ITGB5, ARPC2, ACTR3, TLN1, RHOA, WWTR1, FOSL1 — integrins,
  the Arp2/3 actin-branching complex, talin, RhoA, TAZ and Fra-1. That is the
  adhesion / cytoskeleton / invasion / Hippo axis, which IS the mesenchymal
  programme. Two of them are the canonical published drivers: Bhat et al.
  established TAZ (WWTR1) as the mesenchymal driver in glioma, and Marques et
  al. tied FOSL1 to the NF1-loss axis that defines the subtype. ITGB5 was
  reported in 2019 as "specific to the mesenchymal subtype" in its own right.

  PN came back with KIF18B, KIF2C, VRK1, MCM10, E2F3 — mitotic kinesins, a
  cell-cycle kinase, a replication licensing factor and an E2F transcription
  factor. A proliferating progenitor programme, which is what "proneural
  progenitor" means.

  CL came back with FGFR1 and CDK6. Classical glioblastoma is defined by
  receptor-tyrosine-kinase signalling and CDKN2A loss, and CDK4/6 amplification
  is a known feature of it.

  OLIGO came back with HAPLN2 and ANLN. HAPLN2 builds the extracellular matrix
  at the nodes of Ranvier in myelinated fibres. ANLN is enriched in mature
  myelinating oligodendrocytes, and knocking it out there causes myelin
  outfoldings. Both are textbook myelin genes.

  MTC came back with ATP5MC1, a subunit of mitochondrial ATP synthase — the
  terminal enzyme of oxidative phosphorylation, in the OXPHOS subtype.

None of this was engineered. The engine never saw a pathway name.

The single best individual finding is RPP25L in MTC. A 2022 Society for
Neuro-Oncology report describes glioblastomas that silence RPP25 by promoter
hypermethylation becoming dependent on its paralog RPP25L, with cell lines
lacking RPP25 showing "a rapid reduction in viability upon RPP25L loss",
confirmed in patient-derived neurospheres. That is a published, glioblastoma-
specific synthetic lethality — the same class of target as PRMT5/MTAP — and
this pipeline surfaced it independently.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT THIS IS NOT — READ THIS BEFORE PRESENTING ANY OF IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  · These are CANDIDATES, not treatments. Nothing here has been tested in a
    laboratory by this project.

  · The dependency data come from CELL LINES, not from patients. Cell lines
    lose the immune system, the blood-brain barrier and the tumour
    microenvironment. A mesenchymal target that depends on macrophage
    infiltration cannot show its true importance in a dish at all — which is a
    specific reason the MES panel may be understated.

  · The subtype assignment of the cell lines was NOT used, because DepMap
    expression data was not available. So this measures "glioblastoma-selective"
    dependency, not "MTC-selective" or "MES-selective" dependency. The
    subtype link comes from the patient side only. Downloading
    OmicsExpressionProteinCodingGenesTPMLogp1.csv from depmap.org would let the
    53 glioblastoma lines be classified with our own model and turn this into a
    direct per-subtype dependency test. That is the single biggest available
    upgrade.

  · 40 of the 61 checked genes have NO known drug. A target without a molecule
    is a research programme, not a therapy.

  · Two named drugs against targets on this list have already failed in
    glioblastoma trials.

  HOW TO REPRODUCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    cd 01_Engine
    python3 01_patient_side.py
    python3 02_dependency_side.py
    python3 03_intersect.py
    python3 04_integrate_and_excel.py
    python3 05_plots.py

  Steps 1-3 and 5 are deterministic. Step 4 reads the stored literature file
  rather than searching again, so it reproduces exactly.

  THE ONE-SENTENCE VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  For each of the six subtypes, genes that both identify it in real patients
  and that a glioblastoma cell cannot survive without while normal cells can —
  17 of them with a drug that already exists, 5 thrown out because the
  published literature said inhibiting them would help the tumour, and every
  claim carrying the URL it came from.
